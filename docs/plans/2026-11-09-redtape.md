# Redtape: build plan

**Goal:** Build Redtape as designed in `docs/specs/2026-11-09-redtape-design.md`: one activity and three places
in, a proved side-by-side comparison out, on one Cloudflare Worker, in the look of `DESIGN.md`.

**Stack:** TypeScript (ESM, strict), Hono 4 with `hono/jsx`, Cloudflare Workers, D1, static assets, a cron every
minute, Vitest in the Workers pool, Wrangler 4. Same versions and conventions as `../backed`.

**The model:** `../backed` is a finished product with the same shape (a slow TinyFish run, then a free proof
check, then pages and exports). Read it first. Copy its project files, its fetch interceptor for tests, its
ledger, limits, visitor hash, run slot, `advance` pattern, site shell, exports and tests' style, and change
what the spec changes. Do not copy its product words, its look or its fixtures.

## Constraints

- Tests never reach TinyFish. Every outbound request goes through an interceptor that throws on a request it was
  not told to expect.
- **Fixtures are invented.** Fictional places, authorities, officials and pages (for example "Port Aldren",
  "Aldren Licensing Office", `licensing.aldren.gov.example`), written by hand in the shape of the real
  responses. The real captures in `../spikes/compliance-9nov/` and `../spikes/research-9nov/` are for reading
  and are never copied into this repository. No real person's name in any file.
- Every limit and price lives in `src/core/limits.ts`. Every TinyFish call writes one ledger row.
- The key is only in `.dev.vars` and in the Worker's secrets. It is never printed or logged.
- No paid TinyFish call while building. TinyResearch is never called from a test or by hand during the build.
  Free calls (Fetch, Search) are allowed for looking at real pages.
- The site sits behind `SITE_ENABLED` until it is switched on.
- Copy on the site follows the voice in `DESIGN.md`. The honest-state sentences in the spec are exact.
- Tests cover behaviour that can go wrong (the report reader, the proof check, the figures, the state machine,
  limits, input rules), not markup details. `npm run check` once per task.
- Comments state the rule itself. No file says or implies who or what wrote it.

## Things that are easy to get wrong

1. The report is prose from a model. Every field can be missing, renamed, wrapped in bold, followed by a
   citation number, or put in the wrong place. The reader keeps what it can read and counts what it drops; only
   a report with no categories or a missing place fails the comparison.
2. "not stated" is the only gap. "Free", "none", "0" and "no fee" are stated fees. A fee of "not stated (see the
   fee schedule)" is a gap.
3. The quote check must not pass on a few common words. The run of matching words has a minimum length and a
   minimum share of the quote (spec section 5). A quote of three words passes only whole.
4. An amount is digits: "$300" and "USD 300" and "300 dollars" all carry "300". "1,000" and "1000" are the same
   amount. "3" must not match inside "300".
5. A place is typed by a person and written back by a model: "Dubai mainland (United Arab Emirates)" and
   "Dubai Mainland, UAE" are the same place. Match by the first words, case-insensitively, and never guess
   between two places that start alike: fail instead.
6. `advance` can be called by a page and the cron at the same moment. Each step claims its work in one SQL
   statement. The research run id is stored before the first poll, and a retry never starts a second run.
7. One `advance` does a small, fixed amount of work: at most 4 page reads. A Worker request has limits on
   subrequests and CPU.
8. The activity and the places are shown back on pages and go into a research question. They are escaped on
   output, limited on input, and stripped of line breaks before they go into the question.
9. Currencies are not converted and not added across currencies. "USD 300" plus "USD 23" is "at least USD 323";
   "USD 300" plus "GBP 100" is a list.
10. A page that could not be read is not a page that lacks the sentence. They are different results with
    different words.
